# The Centriole as the Organelle of Irreversible Differentiation: A Multi-Counter Architecture of Replicative Aging

**Jaba Tqemaladze** · Georgia Longevity Alliance · ORCID: 0000-0001-8651-7243

---

## Abstract

Replicative arrest persists when telomeres are maintained and mitochondria are protected. The centriole is the organelle with which irreversible differentiation is associated. In asymmetric stem cell divisions, the old mother centriole is selectively inherited by the daughter that retains stemness (Yamashita et al. 2007; Barandun, Oxenius et al. 2025). With each division, the mother centriole ages. The counter is centriole age. The readout is the ratio of centriole length to cilium length, indexed by polyglutamylation (polyE). When the primary cilium is active, the centriole is short, polyE is low, and the cell is plastic. When the cilium is resorbed, the centriole elongates, polyE accumulates, and the cell commits. polyE reflects the cumulative time the centriole has dominated over the cilium — a structural index of commitment. The centriole anchors cell identity: lose it in a pluripotent cell and pluripotency collapses (Renzova et al. 2018); lose it in a somatic cell and the differentiation lock is lifted. A microprotein at centriole subdistal appendages (miP-FERMT3) directly triggers p53-independent senescence and increases with age (Raheja et al. 2026). Evidence from C. elegans (551/558 cells eliminate centrioles), Drosophila (forced centriole retention breaks totipotency), and planarians (centrosome-free neoblasts) converges on the same principle. I define a falsifiable experiment: centriole elimination → OSKM reprogramming. Prediction: removal of the old centriole increases reprogramming efficiency 5-10 fold.

## 1. The Problem

The centriole oscillates between two states. In its **cilium state,** it is a basal body — short, anchored at the membrane, supporting a primary cilium that receives Hedgehog, Wnt, and other developmental signals. In its **centrosome state,** the cilium is resorbed, the centriole elongates, matures, and organizes the mitotic spindle. These two states are mutually exclusive (Atmakuru & Dhawan 2023, PMID 37144419).

With each division cycle, the centriole spends time in each state. But the balance may shift. Over many divisions, the centriole may spend progressively more time in the centrosomal state and less in the ciliary state. The cumulative ratio — time spent as centrosome vs. time spent as basal body — is recorded as polyglutamylation (polyE) on the centriolar microtubules. polyE accumulates during the centrosomal phase and does not accumulate during the ciliary phase.

hTERT extends the Hayflick limit but does not abolish it (Bodnar et al. 1998, PMID 9454332). 2% O₂ protects mitochondria from oxidative damage (Parrinello et al. 2003, PMID 12855956). With both telomeres and mitochondria shielded, BJ fibroblasts still arrest. Passanisi & Spencer (2026, PMID 41816297) confirmed at single-cell resolution: telomere length, DDR, and oxidation do not distinguish cycling from non-cycling cells.

The centriole — specifically, the progressive shift from its cilium state to its centrosome state — is what counts.

## 2. What Counts: Centriole Age, Read Out as polyE

### 2.1 The counter is centriole age

The mother centriole survives division after division. In asymmetric stem cell divisions, the old mother centriole is inherited by the daughter that retains stem cell identity: the Drosophila germline stem cell (Yamashita et al. 2007, PMID 17255513) and the mammalian CD8⁺ T cell (Barandun, Oxenius et al. 2025, PMID 39764850; ninein-dependent). Each division adds one to the mother centriole's count. Centriole age — measured in divisions survived — is the counter.

### 2.2 polyE is an index of the centriole-to-cilium length ratio

The centriole and the primary cilium are alternative states of the same organelle (Atmakuru & Dhawan 2023, PMID 37144419). When the cilium is present, the centriole functions as a basal body and is structurally short. The cilium serves as the cell's signaling antenna (Hedgehog, Wnt). When the cilium is resorbed, the centriole becomes the centrosome — it elongates, matures, and organizes the mitotic spindle. These two states are mutually exclusive.

polyE accumulates preferentially on centriolar microtubules during the centrosomal phase. It does not accumulate on the basal body during the ciliary phase. The level of polyE therefore reflects the **cumulative time the centriole has spent dominating over the cilium.**

```
PLASTIC STATE                         COMMITTED STATE
(стволовая клетка / необласт)         (дифференцированная клетка)

   ═══ длинная цилия                   ─── короткая / отсутствует
   ─── короткая центриоль              ═══ длинная центриоль
   polyE низкий                        polyE высокий
   центриоль/цилия ↓                   центриоль/цилия ↑
   Wnt/Hh активен                      Wnt/Hh подавлен
   может делиться и                    не может делиться
   дедифференцироваться
```

polyE is not a clock face for centriole age. It is a clock face for the **centriole-to-cilium ratio.** The longer the centriole has dominated, the more polyE it carries. The ratio itself is the structural index of commitment.

This is a novel hypothesis. No direct experimental data exist for the polyE-to-ratio relationship. It is testable: U-ExM measurement of centriole length, cilium length, and polyE intensity in the same cells across passages should reveal a monotonic relationship between the centriole/cilium ratio and polyE.

### 2.3 Why this ratio matters

The centriole-cilium axis controls the cell's access to developmental signals. A cell with a long cilium and short centriole is receptive to Hedgehog, Wnt, and other morphogens — it is in a state of signaling competence. A cell with a long centriole and short or absent cilium is in a state of division competence. The ratio of their lengths records the balance between these two states over the cell's lifetime.

In C. elegans, the 551 somatic cells that eliminate centrioles also lose their cilia. The 7 cells that retain centrioles (B, F, U, Y rectal epithelium; Z2, Z3 germline) are among the few that maintain ciliary function. In planarians, neoblasts are centrosome-free — they have neither centrioles nor cilia — and are maximally plastic. When they differentiate, they acquire centrioles de novo and their plasticity ends.

The ratio hypothesis unifies these observations: commitment is proportional to centriole dominance over cilium. polyE is the biochemical readout of that dominance.

## 3. The Central Experiment

### 3.1 Rationale

If the centriole is the organelle of commitment, removing it from a differentiated cell should remove the commitment. The cell requires a signal to change state. OSKM provides that signal.

**Renzova, Cajanek et al. (2018, PMID 30197118):** PLK4/STIL → centrosome loss in hESCs/hiPSCs → proteasome-mediated degradation of OCT4/NANOG → loss of pluripotency. p53 knockdown did not restore self-renewal — the mechanism is p53-independent. In pluripotent cells, the centriole anchors pluripotency. Removing it collapses pluripotency.

**Raheja, Fleming, Siragusa et al. (2026, PMID 42343301):** miP-FERMT3 localizes to centriole subdistal appendages, colocalizes with ninein and CEP170, induces centrosome amplification, and triggers p53-INDEPENDENT senescence via proteasomal degradation of p21. FERMT3 expression increases with age in mouse and human endothelium. A centriole substructure directly triggers senescence.

**Pimenta-Marques, Bettencourt-Dias et al. (2016, PMID 27229142):** Drosophila oocyte: Polo kinase → PCM degradation → centriole elimination. Blocking this → centrioles retained → sterility. Totipotency requires centriole clearance.

### 3.2 The Renzova paradox resolved

Centriole loss in PSCs → differentiation. Yet we predict centriole loss in somatic cells → facilitated reprogramming. Resolution: the centriole anchors WHATEVER identity the cell currently holds. In a PSC, it anchors pluripotency. In a fibroblast, it anchors the differentiated state. Remove the anchor → the cell is free to respond to signals. The direction depends on which signals are present.

### 3.3 Design

| Step | Procedure | Control |
|:----:|-----------|---------|
| 1 | Centrinone (PLK4i, 100 nM, 3d) OR STIL shRNA (orthogonal approach) | DMSO / siScramble |
| 2 | Confirm >90% acentriolar: IF CP110 + Cep135 + spindle morphology | Untreated |
| 3 | FACS enrichment of Cep135⁻ cells | — |
| 4 | Doxycycline-inducible OSKM + simultaneous centrinone | siScramble + OSKM |
| 5 | **Temporary p53 suppression:** Pifithrin-α or HPV E6 co-expression during centriole loss phase (48h), then washout | p53 shRNA |
| 6 | AP⁺ colony count at day 21 | — |

**Key improvements over previous design:**
(a) **Simultaneous induction.** OSKM begins as centrioles are lost, not after prolonged acentriolar state.
(b) **Temporary p53 suppression.** Pifithrin-α or E6 during the centriole-loss window, then removed. Prevents p53-dependent arrest without permanently suppressing p53.
(c) **Orthogonal approaches.** Centrinone + STIL shRNA independently. PLK4-resistant mutant as specificity control (Renzova 2018, Fig. 4).
(d) **FACS enrichment.** Sorting Cep135⁻ cells before OSKM increases sensitivity.

### 3.4 Predictions

- **Primary:** Centriole elimination → 5-10× increase in iPSC colony formation (from ~30 to 150-500 per 10⁵ cells).
- **Falsification:** If ≤ 2× the control rate, the hypothesis is disconfirmed.
- **If confirmed:** First causal evidence that the centriole is the organelle of commitment.

| C4 | Centriole/cilium morphology | U-ExM | Magiera |
| Divisions | Lineage tracking | ARGUS-LP | Jacquemet |

**Measurement note:** The centriole/cilium ratio requires co-staining of the centriole (SAS-6, Centrin) and the axoneme (acetylated tubulin) in the same U-ExM sample. GT335 (anti-polyE) intensity is measured on the centriole channel. The prediction: polyE intensity per unit centriole length increases monotonically with the centriole/cilium length ratio.

**SILAC-based centriole age measurement (optional, high-risk/high-reward):** Isotopic labeling of centriolar proteins (SAS-6, CEP135) via SILAC in dividing cells. Old centrioles retain old (unlabeled) protein. Mass spectrometry quantifies the fraction of old vs. new protein — a direct measure of centriole age independent of polyE.

## 5. Cross-Species Evidence

### 5.1 C. elegans: elimination locks the centrosome state permanently

671 embryonic cells → 558 at L1. 551 eliminate centrioles. Without a centriole, there is no centrosome state AND no cilium state. The cell is locked in a permanent post-mitotic identity. Only 7 cells retain centrioles: B, F, U, Y rectal epithelial cells + Z2, Z3 germline (Croisier, Gönczy et al. 2025, PMID 40475707; EM). These 7 cells can still oscillate between the two states. Z2/Z3 divide indefinitely — their centrioles continue to cycle between cilium and centrosome states. Adult: 959 somatic cells. Mechanism of elimination unknown (Kalbfuss & Gönczy 2023, PMID 37963546).

### 5.2 Drosophila: forced retention of the old centrosome state breaks totipotency

Polo kinase → PCM degradation → centriole elimination in oocyte. The oocyte must shed the centrosome state entirely to become totipotent. Blocking this elimination → the old centriole remains → sterility (PMID 27229142).

### 5.3 Planarians: neoblasts avoid the centrosome state entirely

Planarians are centrosome-free (Li et al. 2020, PMID 32776587). Neoblasts have neither centrioles nor cilia — they exist outside the centriole/cilium axis. This is what makes them maximally plastic. Centrioles appear de novo only at terminal differentiation. The appearance of a centriole — its first entry into the centrosome state — marks the end of plasticity.

### 5.4 Fertilization: total clearance of both states

Maternal centrioles eliminated. Sperm centrioles provide PCM seed for de novo assembly. Total reset requires total centriole clearance — removing the accumulated history of centrosomal dominance.

### 5.5 Human endothelium
miP-FERMT3 at centriole subdistal appendages → p53-independent senescence. FERMT3 increases with age (Raheja et al. 2026, PMID 42343301).

## 6. Molecular Mechanisms

### 6.1 The centriole-cilium ratio as a structural index of commitment

This is a novel hypothesis. The centriole and primary cilium are the same organelle in two states. When the cilium dominates, the cell signals. When the centriole dominates, the cell divides. With each division cycle, the centriole may spend slightly more time in the centrosomal state and slightly less in the ciliary state. Over many divisions, the cumulative ratio shifts toward centriole dominance. polyE marks this shift.

The mechanism by which the ratio is sensed could involve the subdistal appendages. These structures — which anchor ninein for asymmetric centrosome inheritance — are positioned at the boundary between the centriole and the cilium. A change in the centriole/cilium ratio would alter the geometry of the subdistal appendages. miP-FERMT3 binds precisely at these appendages and triggers senescence. The ratio may be the physical signal that recruits or activates senescence-initiating proteins.

### 6.2 The centriole as the organelle of commitment

The centriole integrates three cell-fate systems: (i) the tubulin code (polyE as cumulative readout of the centriole/cilium ratio); (ii) proteostasis regulation (OCT4/NANOG via Renzova 2018; p21 via Raheja 2026); and (iii) asymmetric inheritance (Yamashita 2007; Barandun 2025). No other organelle integrates these three systems at a single physical location.

### 6.3 Centriole age is structural

Centriole age is not a biochemical modification. It is the number of divisions the organelle has survived. Its microtubules have undergone N cycles of polymerization stress. Its cartwheel has been assembled and disassembled N times via ALMS1-mediated cycles (Ozaki, Tsou et al. 2026, PMID 42380124) — a process the authors explicitly describe as involving "memory." Structural age cannot be erased by modifying polyE.

### 6.4 Centrosome as signaling and proteostasis hub

PLK1, Aurora A, p53 concentrated at the centrosome (Nigg et al. 2014, PMID 25047618). miP-FERMT3 binding at subdistal appendages triggers p21 degradation and p53-independent senescence. The centriole actively regulates the stability of cell-identity factors.

## 8. Limitations

1. **No direct experimental data.** Extrapolation from 4 model systems. Central experiment not performed.
2. **polyE-to-ratio hypothesis is novel and untested.** Direct experimental validation required.
3. **PLK4 pleiotropy.** Mitigated by STIL shRNA + resistant mutant approaches.
4. **p53-dependent arrest upon centriole loss.** Mitigated by temporary p53 suppression during the loss window.
5. **C. elegans centriole map incomplete** (L1 only).
6. **Tissue-level extrapolation requires the Centriole Map Initiative.**

## 9. Discussion

MCARA identifies the centriole — specifically, its progressive shift from the cilium state to the centrosome state — as the basis of irreversible differentiation. The counter is centriole age. The readout is the centriole-to-cilium length ratio, indexed by polyE. Asymmetric inheritance transmits the old centriole to the stem cell, ensuring that the organelle with the longest centrosomal history remains with the cell that must preserve identity.

The ratio hypothesis provides a unified explanation across four phyla. In C. elegans, centriole elimination removes the centrosome state AND the cilium state, locking the cell in permanent post-mitotic identity. In Drosophila oocytes, forced retention of the old centrosome state prevents the transition to totipotency. In planarians, neoblasts avoid commitment by avoiding the centrosome/cilium axis entirely. In human cells, miP-FERMT3 demonstrates that a protein binding to centriole subdistal appendages — structures positioned precisely at the boundary between the centriole and cilium states — can directly trigger senescence. FERMT3 expression increases with age, suggesting that the balance between the two states shifts over a lifetime.

The framework explains why partial reprogramming fails: the epigenome resets, but the old centriole remains. Its accumulated history of centrosomal dominance — reflected in its length, its polyE, its centriole/cilium ratio — anchors the old identity. Full rejuvenation requires centriolar reset: restoring the cilium state.

The central experiment is clean and falsifiable. Predicted effect: 5-10× increase in iPSC efficiency. Either outcome advances the field.
