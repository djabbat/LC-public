Two preprints, both short.

**MCARA** — the centriole as a gatekeeper of cell state. A microtubule-based organelle that can't repair itself. It has two states: centrosome organizer (entropy accumulates, polyglutamylation rises as compensation) and cilium organizer (accumulation stops, signaling mode). Over time, with every division, the balance tips irreversibly toward senescence or differentiation.

**CEDAR** — the experiment. Eliminate centrioles. Then reprogram with Yamanaka factors. If the centriole is a physical lock on cell fate, removing it should raise reprogramming efficiency at least twofold. If it drops, I'm wrong. Nobody has done this. Sixty verified PMIDs, nothing. Thirteen groups, every outcome informative.

(The preprint says OSKM. My thinking has moved further — DUX4 instead of Yamanaka, then de novo centriole assembly to lock in the new state. Eliminate, reprogram, rebuild. Happy to discuss.)

Nature clears centrioles at fertilization — the zygote builds them de novo. If biology wants a clean slate, it clears the centrioles first.
