A quick follow-up, if I may. Your note that fertilised zygotes are "needles in the haystack amongst millions of yolk cells inside the egg cocoons" raised a question that I should have asked in my first message.

What happens to the fertilised zygotes that do not develop into juveniles? In many invertebrates, excess embryos are resorbed or serve as nutrition for the surviving ones within the cocoon. Is this the case in Schmidtea — do the yolk cells actively eliminate supernumerary embryos, or do the zygotes simply compete for a finite nutrient supply?

Put differently: is the number of emerging juveniles limited by cocoon resources, or is there an active programme of embryo elimination?

This is a side question, not central to my centriole interest — but I find myself curious. If the answer is, once again, "nobody has looked," I will take it as a pattern rather than a disappointment.
