# Abstract for bioRxiv (BIORXIV/2026/743702) — HTML-entity версия

Background. The central unresolved problem in rejuvenation research is why partial reprogramming (OSK) resets epigenetic clocks yet does not restore tissue function (Y&#252;cel &amp; Gladyshev, 2024). We hypothesize the centriole as a candidate hardware component that OSK cannot reset: a stem-cell division limit persists despite interventions that mitigate each known hallmark (telomerase, hypoxia, epigenetic reset, mitophagy, senolytics). Telomeres, the epigenome, and mitochondria have documented reset mechanisms; the centriole has none documented (actively searched negative; reversibility open). This is a low-certainty, falsifiable hypothesis (no direct HSC data); its value is a pre-registered protocol (P1&#8211;P9) that can upgrade or refute it.

Methods. We performed a systematic review with stratified qualitative&#8211;quantitative evidence synthesis (eight databases, 1965&#8211;2026; 1,998 records screened, 79 included; &#954; = 0.81; I&#178;/&#964;&#178;/Q, Egger/Begg), a genome/structural screen of tubulin-code enzymes, and calibrated a saturating drift&#8211;autoregressive model of centriole-geometry transmission (Monte-Carlo and Bayesian; posterior predictive check; directionally consistent with&#8212;but not validated by&#8212;independent FRAC data). Confidence is assessed by a GRADE-CERQual-adapted rubric and Bradford Hill criteria; the model is a hypothesis-generation tool, not an empirical result.

Results. Telomeres are reset by telomerase/ZSCAN4, the epigenome partially by OSK, mitochondria during iPSC reprogramming (PINK1-dependent). Centriole geometry changes with age (FRAC R = &#8722;0.66; +29% over-elongation; 3&#215; amplification), is inherited spatially, and causes senescence in vitro (NEDD1 depletion). The calibrated model (&#945; prior-informed 0.964; &#956; = 0.0292/division; posterior predictive p = 0.496) reproduces the +29% elongation but not the magnitude of the FRAC correlation (&#8722;0.66); it is therefore directionally consistent but quantitatively unvalidated. No somatic in-place repair of wall PTMs was found in 79 studies (actively searched negative; reversibility open).

Conclusions. The centriole merits investigation as a candidate physical substrate for division counting in centriole-bearing proliferating stem cells, distinguished by a wall for which no in-place repair has been documented (actively searched negative; reversibility open). This is a hypothesis-generating synthesis with a pre-registered falsification protocol (P1&#8211;P9) that can upgrade or refute it; no primary HSC data are presented, and Stage 1 status is declared. The hypothesis is provisional, species-scoped, and falsifiable: P1&#8211;P9 answer the open questions with pre-specified thresholds.
