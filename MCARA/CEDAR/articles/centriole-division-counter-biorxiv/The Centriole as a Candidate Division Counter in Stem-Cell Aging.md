# The Centriole as a Candidate Division Counter in Stem-Cell Aging: A Falsifiable Hypothesis with a Pre-Registered Protocol

**Author**: Jaba Tqemaladze
**Affiliation**: Agricultural University of Georgia
**Correspondence**: jaba@longevity.ge | **ORCID**: 0000-0001-8651-7243
**Article type**: Research Article — hypothesis-driven comparative synthesis with quantitative thresholds, stochastic modeling, and a pre-registered falsification protocol (P1–P9); no primary HSC data are presented (Stage 1 status declared).

## Abstract

**Background.** The central unresolved problem in rejuvenation research is why partial reprogramming (OSK) resets epigenetic clocks yet does not restore tissue function (Yücel & Gladyshev, 2024). We hypothesize the centriole as a candidate hardware component that OSK cannot reset: a stem-cell division limit persists despite interventions that mitigate each known hallmark (telomerase, hypoxia, epigenetic reset, mitophagy, senolytics). Telomeres, the epigenome, and mitochondria have documented reset mechanisms; the centriole has none documented (actively searched negative; reversibility open). This is a low-certainty, falsifiable hypothesis (no direct HSC data); its value is a pre-registered protocol (P1–P9) that can upgrade or refute it.

**Methods.** We performed a systematic review with stratified qualitative–quantitative evidence synthesis (eight databases, 1965–2026; 1,998 records screened, 79 included; κ = 0.81; I²/τ²/Q, Egger/Begg), a genome/structural screen of tubulin-code enzymes, and calibrated a saturating drift–autoregressive model of centriole-geometry transmission (Monte-Carlo and Bayesian; posterior predictive check; directionally consistent with—but not validated by—independent FRAC data). Confidence is assessed by a GRADE-CERQual-adapted rubric and Bradford Hill criteria; the model is a hypothesis-generation tool, not an empirical result.

**Results.** Telomeres are reset by telomerase/ZSCAN4, the epigenome partially by OSK, mitochondria during iPSC reprogramming (PINK1-dependent). Centriole geometry changes with age (FRAC R = −0.66; +29% over-elongation; 3× amplification), is inherited spatially, and causes senescence in vitro (NEDD1 depletion). The calibrated model (α prior-informed 0.964; μ = 0.0292/division; posterior predictive p = 0.496) reproduces the +29% elongation but not the magnitude of the FRAC correlation (−0.66); it is therefore directionally consistent but quantitatively unvalidated. No somatic in-place repair of wall PTMs was found in 79 studies (actively searched negative; reversibility open).

**Conclusions.** The centriole merits investigation as a candidate physical substrate for division counting in centriole-bearing proliferating stem cells, distinguished by a wall for which no in-place repair has been documented (actively searched negative; reversibility open). This is a hypothesis-generating synthesis with a pre-registered falsification protocol (P1–P9) that can upgrade or refute it; no primary HSC data are presented, and Stage 1 status is declared. The hypothesis is provisional, species-scoped, and falsifiable: P1–P9 answer the open questions with pre-specified thresholds.

**Keywords:** replicative senescence, hallmarks of aging, centriole geometry, centriole inheritance, tubulin PTMs, de novo assembly, telomeres, epigenetic clock, mtDNA, stem cell aging

## 1. Introduction

Replicative senescence (Hayflick & Moorhead, 1961; Hayflick, 1965) is one facet of cellular senescence (Campisi & d'Adda di Fagagna, 2007); this work proposes the centriole as a candidate mechanism for the residual division limit that persists when every known hallmark of aging is mitigated, scoped to replicative senescence in centriole-bearing proliferating stem cells. The hypothesis derives from the author's earlier work (Tkemaladze & Chichinadze, 2005; Chichinadze et al., 2008, 2012; Tqemaladze, 2023; disclosed in CoI, not cited as evidence); the present work adds formalization, quantitative synthesis, and a falsifiable protocol. The systematic question: **which of the four proposed counters can be reset by known mechanisms, and which cannot?**

The authoritative Hallmarks of Stem Cell Aging (Rando et al., 2025) do not list the centriole among drivers—a gap this work addresses. Mitigating each known hallmark leaves a division limit that still occurs (Allsopp et al., 2001, 2003; Parrinello et al., 2003; Hickson et al., 2019; Yücel & Gladyshev, 2024). Two logical limits are stated: (i) "no somatic reset is reported" is absence of reports, not evidence of absence—**the centriole is the least studied counter, not the least resettable one**; (ii) every mitigation is partial, so the residual limit could reflect incomplete interventions—tested by the combined-intervention arm (Stage A). Leading alternatives (DNA damage, proliferative stress, proteostasis failure) are developed in §5.3. Within these limits, we hypothesize (not assert) that the centriole is a candidate limit that none of these interventions resets and that has not been directly tested.

**Terminology.** The centrosome = two centrioles + pericentriolar material (PCM); the centriole proper is the nine-triplet wall carrying tubulin PTMs. PCM is a phase-separated condensate (Woodruff et al., 2017); wall tubulin shows slow, cap-controlled plus-end elongation (Iyer & Akhmanova, 2026). Claims of irreversibility refer to the **wall**. Reset is defined by a shared functional criterion—restoration of proliferative and differentiation capacity toward young-cell levels, partial recovery allowed. We distinguish **repair in situ** from **elimination + de novo synthesis**. Per counter: telomeres—in situ elongation (telomerase/ZSCAN4); epigenome—in situ demethylation (OSK, partial); mitochondria—iPSC-driven rejuvenation (PINK1-dependent); centriole—reset would require wall-PTM reversal in situ (absent) or elimination + de novo (germline-exclusive). The claim: no physiologically established somatic mechanism for complete centriole-wall rejuvenation was identified—an **active negative search result** (CCP1–6, TTL, HDAC6/SIRT2, centriole turnover): all documented reversal reactions act on soluble, cytoplasmic, or axonal tubulin; none on the mature centriolar wall in situ.

## 2. Methods

**Search** (conducted 7–8 August 2026): PubMed (1,241), WoS+Scopus (412), EMBASE (128), BIOSIS (87), Cochrane (24), Google Scholar (160, suppl.), bioRxiv/medRxiv (33, suppl.); citation chasing. Example PubMed string (full strings in OSF): ("centriole" OR "centrosome")[Title/Abstract] AND (aging OR senescence OR replicative OR turnover OR repair OR homeostasis OR inheritance OR elongation OR polyglutamylation OR detyrosination OR "Δ2-tubulin") AND (1965:2026[dp]).

**Screening (PRISMA 2020):** 1,998 identified → 438 full-text → 79 included. Inclusion: primary studies with quantitative centriolar/centrosomal measures (length, geometry, PTM, amplification, inheritance) in any cell type, or quantitative reset evidence for telomeres/epigenome/mitochondria; any species; 1965–2026. Exclusion: non-quantitative reviews/opinions/case reports; duplicate reviews; insufficient variance. Limitations: single primary screener with 20% independent duplicate (κ = 0.81); **two independent screeners with 100% blind duplication and third-party adjudication are planned before submission of any Stage 2 results and will be applied retroactively to the present synthesis upon revision**. RoB: SYRCLE/QUADAS-2 (OSF); sensitivity analysis excluding high-RoB studies pre-specified.

**Evidence-confidence rubric.** GRADE-CERQual-adapted for mechanistic data: each claim rated on method-appropriateness, coherence, adequacy, relevance. Telomere reset: high; centriolar age-change: moderate (four cell types); centriolar causation: low (in vitro only); HSC-counter claims: unrated (no data). The central hypothesis is evaluated by Bradford Hill criteria and falsifiability (P1–P9), not by GRADE (inapplicable to hypotheses).

**Heterogeneity (quantified, stratified).** Pooled length I² = 88% (τ² = 0.021), PTM I² = 76%, inheritance I² = 64%; Q = 142.3/38.7; Egger p = 0.29, Begg τ = 0.11 (exploratory only at k = 4–6, underpowered). Within plasma-cell studies I² = 41%; sperm-FRAC 29%. **No pooled cross-stratum estimate is used in calibration**; narrative synthesis (Hartung–Knapp) for integration; cross-platform pooling is not performed because platform heterogeneity (I² > 75%) makes it invalid. **Meta-regression:** underpowered at k = 4–6; heterogeneity sources are assessed qualitatively (tissue, platform, pathology), and a meta-regression is pre-specified on the post-P1 replication set (≥10 studies), with residual heterogeneity > 50% triggering a formal re-appraisal of synthesis validity.

**Transcriptomics.** GSE104406/GSE59114, 127 centriole genes (DESeq2, BH-FDR < 0.05); n = 10/group detects |log₂FC| ≈ 0.8; **underpowered and non-HSC-specific—no conclusion is drawn from it and it is not used in the main argument**; deferred to HSC-specific resources (Sun et al., 2014; Adelman et al., 2019) and scRNA-seq.

**Genome/structural screen.** Ensembl (human, mouse, bovine) of TTL; TTLL1–13 (TTLL12 required for ciliary axoneme—Ceglowski et al., 2024); CCP1–6 (CCP5 loss perturbs spermatogenesis—Giordano et al., 2019); VASH/SVBP; ATAT1; HDAC6; SIRT2; SPAST; human centrosome proteome (Danielsson et al., 2020); TTL (Szyk et al., 2011) and CCP5 (Chen et al., 2024) structures.

**Stochastic model.** Lₙ₊₁ = α·Lₙ + (1−α)·L₀ + μ·(1 − Lₙ/L_max) + εₙ (drift-augmented AR(1); Euler–Maruyama of dL = [−θ(L−L₀) + μ·(1 − L/L_max)]dt + σdW; saturation prevents unbounded growth; a linear-drift variant was rejected as it predicts unbounded elongation). Calibration: Monte-Carlo (2,000 runs), median L₅₀ = 1.291 (SD 0.099) at α = 0.964, μ = 0.0292; Var(L∞) = σ²/(1−α²). **Reducibility to a generic-damage model:** with one observable (Δ2 or length), a generic one-component accumulation model is formally indistinguishable from the centriolar model; discrimination is structural, not by curve shape: (i) accumulator location (wall vs DNA/proteins—PLA/ET, P1); (ii) non-exchangeability (SILAC t½, P2); (iii) inheritance (mother–daughter correlation, P1); (iv) reset differential (P5). Normalized damage scale D_i ∈ [0, 1] permits cross-counter comparison; MCARA predicts dD_centriole/dt_reset = 0 vs > 0 for the others—the falsifiable signature. All model outputs are interpretive (formally Discussion), not part of the empirical Results.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Telomeres (evidence confidence: high)

50–200 bp lost per division (Harley et al., 1990). hTERT extends lifespan (Bodnar et al., 1998; Yang et al., 1999; Tomás-Loba et al., 2008). ZSCAN4 elongates telomeres via H3K14ac/H3K18ac; KO shortens them (Meltzer et al., 2024; Dan et al., 2017). **Scope caveat:** ZSCAN4-mediated elongation is documented in mouse embryonic stem cells, not in HSC; it is cited only to show that a stem-cell-context reset exists—the HSC argument rests on telomerase (Allsopp et al., 2001, 2003), not on ZSCAN4. **Resettable; oncogenic risk context-dependent.** Yet in HSCs, telomerase activity coexists with exhaustion in serial transplantation—telomere reset alone does not remove the limit.

### 3.2 Epigenome (evidence confidence: moderate)

353-CpG clock, 3.6-y error (Horvath, 2013); 71-CpG blood clock (Hannum et al., 2013). OSKM erases most marks with residual lineage memory (Takahashi & Yamanaka, 2006; pluripotency transition—Jia et al., 2024); embryonic genome activation (DUX4/MERVL; Hendrickson et al., 2017) illustrates the depth of the reset; OSK reverses the clock in vivo via TET1/TET2/TDG (Lu et al., 2020). **Resettable (partial).** OSK reverses the clock but not tissue function (Yücel & Gladyshev, 2024).

### 3.3 Mitochondria (evidence confidence: moderate)

iPSC reprogramming restores embryonic-like morphology and ΔΨm (Suhr et al., 2010); PINK1-dependent—KO reduces efficiency ~5-fold (0.3% → 0.06%; Vázquez-Martín et al., 2016). **Scope caveat:** the PINK1-dependent rejuvenation was demonstrated in fibroblasts; its operation in HSC is an extrapolation, not a demonstrated fact. Tissue heteroplasmy segregation is selection, not reset (Lechuga-Vieco et al., 2020); telomere and mitochondrial replication stress share G4/D-loop origins (Billard & Poncet, 2019); SCNT links counters but is extreme (Samiec & Skrzyszowska, 2021). **Resettable during reprogramming.** With hypoxia, three of four counters reset—the limit persists.

### 3.4 Centriole (no somatic reset reported—hypothesis, not established fact)

**Damage type I—chemical (wall PTMs).** The tubulin code (Janke & Magiera, 2020; McKenna et al., 2023): acetylation, detyrosination, and physiological polyglutamylation are reversible; Δ2-tubulin and C-terminal truncation have no known reverse reaction. The centriolar code is incompletely characterized (Guichard et al., 2023); glutamylation decorates centriole tubulin (Bobinnec et al., 1998b); centrioles can be disassembled in vivo (Bobinnec et al., 1998a). **Centriole maturation is dynamic, not static** (Sullenberger et al., 2020), stability is ensured also by structural proteins (Poc1, centrin, SAS-6), and centrosomal proteins undergo differential turnover (Nup188 levels at centrosomes are turnover-controlled; Vishnoi et al., 2020)—so protein-level exchange is documented, and the counter claim is restricted to the wall-PTM layer, with P1 including structural markers and P2 measuring wall-subunit exchange directly. Δ2 forms via vasohibin/SVBP carboxypeptidases (Aillaud et al., 2017) and accumulates in stable assemblies (Paturle-Lafanechère et al., 1994); PTMs stabilize microtubules during morphogenesis (Bulinski & Gundersen, 1991); re-tyrosination requires TTL, whose active site needs the terminal tyrosine and adjacent glutamate (Szyk et al., 2011)—both absent in Δ2. The structural basis is demonstrated for soluble recombinant tubulin in vitro; extrapolation to the closed centriolar wall is unverified (vasohibin/SVBP and CCP carboxypeptidases act on polymerized microtubules in vivo; Aillaud et al., 2017), enzyme accessibility to the wall is open (PLA P1; TTL microinjection). Acetylation (Lys40/ATAT1) protects long-lived microtubules against mechanical ageing (Portran et al., 2017), reversed by HDAC6/SIRT2—an adaptive layer, excluded from the counter. Polyglutamylation is reversible on soluble tubulin (Audebert et al., 1993) and actively regulated in vivo (CCP1 loss enhances polyglutamylation; Pan et al., 2026; Δ2 generation enzyme-controlled—AGTPBP1 mutation causes sperm defects; Wang et al., 2026)—excluded from the counter (Table 2). **Terminality is restricted to Δ2 and C-terminal truncation (Table 2), provisionally**, resting on structural constraints (TTL cannot accommodate Δ2; Szyk et al., 2011) and the absence of a documented reverse enzyme. **'Not studied' vs 'studied and not found':** the active negative search distinguishes enzyme classes tested on centriolar walls in situ (none found acting) from classes never tested on walls (accessibility open; P1/P3); the claim is limited to the first category, the second assigned to P2/P3.

**Damage type II—geometric.** (1) Spatial inheritance: SAS-6 templated by the mother's lumen (Wang et al., 2014); iris-diaphragm template (Albrecht-Buehler, 1990); instructive role of the mother (Feldman et al., 2007). (2) Propagation without correction: rcd4-null Drosophila daughters inherit reduced triplet number; Ana1-driven splaying (62%) propagates (Panda et al., 2024). (3) Molecular candidates: CP110/CEP97/CPAP gate plus-end elongation (Iyer & Akhmanova, 2026); centriole duplication is once-per-cycle, driven by PLK4 (Habedanck et al., 2005; Nigg & Holland, 2018); CPAP excess lengthens (Tang et al., 2009) while CP110 acts antagonistically—the drift μ reflects a disturbed CPAP/CP110 balance, testable by CPAP/CP110 quantification in aged HSC.

**Evidence stratified by strength.** (a) *Correlational*: FRAC R = −0.66 with age (Turner et al., 2021); plasma-cell +29% over-elongation with elevated CPAP (Köhrer et al., 2023); endothelium modification (Bystrevskaya et al., 1992); centrosome aberrations accumulate in cellular aging (Ohshima & Seyama, 2010); 3× amplification in aged Drosophila ISC, metformin-reversible (Park et al., 2014; Na et al., 2015). (b) *Causation in vitro/in vivo*: NEDD1 depletion → centrosome fragmentation → MEF senescence (Manning & Kumar, 2010); **excess centrosomes induce p53-dependent senescence without DNA damage in endothelial cells (Yu et al., 2017)**—a direct causal link from centrosome status to senescence; Odf2 loss → no appendages/cilia (Ishikawa et al., 2005); prolonged mitosis over-elongates centrioles (Kong et al., 2020). (b2) *In vivo association in stem cells*: centrosome misorientation increases with age in Drosophila GSC and reduces stem cell division (Cheng et al., 2008). (c) *SCNT as illustration of resettability, not causal evidence*: the donor centriole is not transferred (Sun & Schatten, 2007); the centrosome–aneuploidy correlation (Dai et al., 2006) is excluded from the causation hierarchy (confounders: imprinting, heteroplasmy, epigenetic errors) and used only to illustrate that elimination + de novo is viable. (d) *Hypothetical*: retained-centriole geometric drift.

**Model-based analysis (interpretive, unvalidated; formally Discussion).** α is postulated (prior 0.9–0.97 templating), measured by P1 (ET ≥50 pairs, r > 0.7); likelihood profiling is flat for α ≥ 0.90—α not identified by Köhrer. μ is the single free parameter, calibrated on Köhrer (+29%/50 divisions): μ = 0.0292, reproducing +29% (L₅₀ = 1.291). **Two-model distinction:** a myeloma-calibrated model (oncogenic context) vs a hypothetical HSC model (μ_HSC unknown; wide prior). The calibrated model is **not** valid for HSC until direct data exist. Sensitivity sweep (α 0.50–0.995): median L₅₀ ≈ 1.29; Turner sign preserved at every α. Bayesian calibration: prior α ~ Beta(90, 3) gives posterior α = 0.964, 95% HDI [0.930, 0.995]; posterior predictive p = 0.496 (calibration, not validation). WAIC = 1.93 (drift) < 3.58 (no-accumulation) < 11.77 (random walk); the three nested models are: drift-augmented AR(1), plain AR(1) without drift, and pure random walk; exploratory (AIC/LOO-CV assigned to post-P1). **Bayes factor:** BF₁₀ is computable but unstable with one dataset; deferred to post-P1 (≥3 datasets stabilize it). **α-identification caveat:** with flat likelihood (ΔlogL < 0.5 for α ≥ 0.90), the posterior for α is fully prior-driven; the posterior mean α = 0.964 is therefore a restatement of the Beta(90, 3) prior, not a data estimate. Overfitting response: 1 parameter, 1 point (df = 0); every other comparison is strictly out-of-sample—the FRAC mismatch is reported rather than tuned away. **Hierarchical structure:** μ_plasma, μ_fibroblast, μ_HSC share a Gamma hyper-prior (shape 1.5, rate 50). Calibration priority: P1-on-fibroblasts (Parrinello et al., 2003); the myeloma value is an upper bound (p53/RAS mutations alter centriole biology). Turner comparison: model predicts R ≈ −0.05…−0.26 vs empirical −0.66—directionally consistent but quantitatively unvalidated; FRAC is a surrogate in post-mitotic sperm. HSC consistency check (NOT calibration): ~50–200 divisions per lifetime (Allsopp et al., 2001, 2003); limiting exhaustion at L ≈ 1.3–1.4 would require μ ≈ 0.03–0.05—order-of-magnitude only. Stress: μ(n) = μ₀ + μ_s·S(n)—NAC identifies μ_s. Blind validation of the μ-regulator axis: CPAP overexpression lengthens (Tang et al., 2009); CPAP loss disrupts cilia (Wu & Tang, 2012); C2CD3 loss shortens (Thauvin-Robinet et al., 2014). **Inheritance-direction parameter s ∈ {+1, −1}:** direction is tissue-specific (Drosophila GSC retain the mother—Yamashita et al., 2007; neuroblasts the daughter—Januschke et al., 2011; mouse neural progenitors the mother—Wang et al., 2009). s = +1 accumulates damage in the stem line; s = −1 exports the old centriole. For HSC, s is unknown (assigned to P1); predictions are reported for both directions, with a pre-specified invariance criterion (conclusions accepted only if they hold under both s values). The daughter is templated on the mother's geometry (α ≈ 0.9–0.97), so errors are copied each assembly regardless of direction—direction modulates rate, not existence.

**Testable corollary.** Protective PTMs rise early and plateau; terminal PTMs rise monotonically with divisions (P1 time-resolved PLA)—a falsifiable distinction between adaptation and damage.

## 4. Pre-Registered Protocol and Predictions

**Pre-registered predictions.** P1: mother–daughter length correlation r > 0.7 (ET, ≥50 pairs/age) and determination of s. P2: length variance increases with age (Levene). P3: frequency of centrioles > 2 SD rises with age. P4: such HSCs show reduced EB1-GFP nucleation. P5 (falsifying; de novo control added): after full OSK reprogramming of aged HSCs, Δ2/geometric anomalies persist on retained centrioles (no wall reset); conversely, engineered centriole replacement should leave the epigenetic clock running—the counter-independence test.

**De novo-assembly control (mandatory).** iPSC reprogramming can dismantle and reassemble centrioles, so Δ2-persistence after OSK could reflect de novo assembly rather than retention. Control: ≥200 centrioles/group/time-point; provenance by markers—ninein (mother appendages), centrin-3 (all), GCP6/PLK4 (recent assembly), time-resolved Δ2. Classification: Δ2 on ninein-positive (retained) = support; Δ2 only on GCP6-positive (de novo) = refute. Maturation-latency: newly assembled centrioles acquire Δ2 only after maturation, whereas a retained centriole already carries Δ2 at t = 0 post-OSK = persistence. **Simplified optional control (PA-GFP-centrin):** photoactivatable GFP–centrin is activated before OSK and its fate tracked; activated (pre-OSK) centrioles persisting with Δ2 = direct persistence proof without STED/EM inference; only unactivated (post-OSK) centrioles showing Δ2 = de novo favored (photoactivation scored successful in ≥10% of cells). **Gene-expression confounder control (RNA-seq/ATAC-seq):** OSK alters expression of centriolar genes (CPAP, CP110, PLK4), so Δ2 persistence could reflect incomplete expression reprogramming; persistence is scored only if centriolar-gene expression and chromatin are reprogrammed to young-like levels (|log₂FC| < 0.5 and ATAC accessibility restored) while Δ2/geometry anomalies persist; otherwise the persistence claim is not made.

**Division-normalized Δ2 (critical).** OSK slows division, so Δ2 persistence could reflect fewer completed cycles. Two controls: (i) division normalization—Δ2 intensity per completed cell cycle (EdU + pH3 mitotic tracking, supplemented by live FUCCI reporters); (ii) matched-division-count comparison at equal cumulative divisions; (iii) division-independent control—mitomycin-C-arrested HSC, to separate Δ2 dilution by division from genuine reset.

**Gated prerequisites (#1 is critical).** (i) PLA Δ2 on young vs aged HSC centrioles (2–3 months, mouse); C57BL/6J, 3 age groups, balanced sex (n = 20/age), ≥200 centrioles/group, ninein/centrin-3 markers, blinded; primary endpoint: Δ2/centrin-3 per centriole volume; hierarchical statistics: animal = unit, mixed-effects model (Y = β₀ + β₁·Age + u_animal + u_cell + ε), with an a priori ICC estimate to justify the animal-level n; a feasibility pilot on 2–3 mice/age precedes the full arm and supplies the ICC and effect-size anchors. (ii) Wall-turnover measurement in proliferating HSCs: SILAC/¹³C-tubulin pulse-chase, or—for slowly dividing HSC—bioorthogonal labeling (azide-tubulin, 48–72 h pulse) with STED-resolved wall incorporation. (iii) ET of ≥50 mother–daughter pairs per age (U-ExM screen first).

**Stage A (model).** Dermal fibroblasts at 3% vs 21% O₂; key arm—hTERT-immortalized fibroblasts with OSK + mitophagy + senolytics simultaneously, Δ2/geometry at terminal passage; prevention arm (CRISPR TTLL1-KO). **Stage B (P5):** OSK with centriole-persistence readout. **Stage C (causal loss–restoration):** Centrinone-B in aged HSC—transient loss → division/senescence markers; washout → restoration → division recovery. Loss→arrest→restoration→rescue is the decisive causal chain; loss-without-recovery refutes the centriole. **Response heterogeneity (pre-specified):** centrinone produces senescent-like and proliferating subpopulations (Yu et al., 2017)—recovery is scored per lineage. **Interpretation boundary:** Centrinone-B tests *necessity*, not *sufficiency*. **p53 confounder control:** arrest can reflect the p53 response to centrosome loss per se (Fong et al., 2016; Meitinger et al., 2016); PFT-α discriminates; karyotype (CIN route; Marteil et al., 2018) is the second control. **Sufficiency test (only route to causation):** replacing the aged wall—cell fusion or microinjection of a young centriole; if replacement does not restore division, the damaged wall is not the limiting factor. **P6 (reprogramming-barrier test, four groups):** (1) Centrinone-B alone (arrest control); (2) Centrinone-B + PFT-α; (3) Centrinone-B + PFT-α + OSK; (4) OSK alone; iPSC-colony efficiency as endpoint. The hypothesis is supported only if a gain appears in group 3 *and* correlates with Δ2/anomaly clearance; a gain in group 2 alone indicates removal of a p53-dependent reprogramming barrier (p53 is itself a centrosome-associated mitotic sensor; Contadini et al., 2019), not of a division counter; laser ablation is the p53-independent alternative. **P7 (proteostasis control):** HSF1-driven chaperone induction in Stage A with Δ2 readout—Δ2 falling under maximal proteostasis marks the centriolar change as secondary; unchanged Δ2 marks it as autonomous. **P8 (epigenetic-counter comparison):** in the same OSK-treated cells, H3K4me2 demethylation and Δ2 are tracked together at 0, 24, 48, 72 h; Δ2 disappearing only after H3K4me2 recovery indicates epigenetic control of the centriolar change, while Δ2 persistence with reset marks favors the centriolar counter. **P9 (acentriolar control):** Centrinone-B-depleted (48 h) vs centriole-intact HSC under OSK—higher iPSC efficiency in depleted cells supports the centriole-as-barrier reading.

**Key thresholds.** PLA asymmetry index > 1.5; **epitope-accessibility control (mandatory):** dense packing may hide Δ2 and cytoplasmic microtubules confound—PLA is complemented by direct STED and a nocodazole control; wall localization is claimed only if Δ2 is on centrin-3+ structures after microtubule depolymerization; SILAC t½ > 10 cycles (support) vs < 2 (refute); ET r > 0.7; P5: Δ2 persists (support) vs cleared (refute); TTL microinjection: **tests wall accessibility, not enzyme capability—the structural block is documented only for soluble tubulin (Szyk et al., 2011); Δ2 cleared in the wall (refute) vs unchanged (support)**; ESC centrioles near-zero Δ2 expected. **P1 ratio readout (maturation vs damage):** Δ2/acetylation co-staining; damage predicts the ratio rising with divisions, maturation predicts proportional rise. Statistics: n ≥ 70/group (n ≥ 30/age for 80% power at ICC ≈ 0.1; final n pre-registered after the pilot), effect sizes pre-specified—PLA asymmetry (d = 0.67), ET (r > 0.7), variance (Levene; d = 0.5), anomaly (OR 2.0), EB1-GFP (d = 0.6). Correlated-endpoint correction: m_eff ≈ 3 (pilot-validated) preserves power (0.96 at n = 70; m_eff = 5 still gives 0.90); group-sequential with one interim analysis (O'Brien–Fleming), intention-to-treat, blinded. **Secondary endpoints:** all secondary endpoints are reported as exploratory with nominal p-values, outside the primary FDR control.

**Discrimination controls.** Hedgehog-rescue (Smo-CA), cilium-resorption (Aurora-A inhibitor), IFT88-knockdown, ARL13B marker, PFT-α stress control, collagen/mtDNA damage-shadow assays.

**Scenario analysis (pre-specified) + decision tree.** P1/P3 negative → centriolar counter refuted for HSC; residual limit attributed to alternatives. P2 negative (wall exchanges) → terminality refuted, geometric counter may remain. P5 Δ2 cleared after OSK → reset exists, hypothesis refuted. P6 no efficiency gain → centriole is not a reprogramming barrier. Mixed results → multi-criteria Bayesian analysis: a Bayes factor is computed against each alternative with **weights and BF thresholds fixed a priori before data collection** (not post-hoc); conclusions are reported as posterior probabilities, not verdicts. All negative → the centriole is not a division counter in HSC; contribution = evidence map + falsification protocol.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Candidate physical substrate for counting

Geometry changes measurably with age (Turner et al., 2021; Köhrer et al., 2023; Uzbekov & Avidor-Reiss, 2024); terminal wall PTMs lack reverse enzymes (provisional); geometry is inherited without correction. Analogy (with caveat): nuclear pore complexes deteriorate in post-mitotic cells yet renew in proliferating ones (D'Angelo et al., 2009)—centrioles, by contrast, do not renew in proliferating stem cells. Telomerase-positive stem cells still exhaust (Allsopp et al., 2001, 2003); hypoxia favors HSC self-renewal but does not abolish the ceiling (Danet et al., 2003); OSK resets the clock without restoring tissue function (Yücel & Gladyshev, 2024)—implying a non-telomeric, non-epigenetic structural counter. Falsifiable premise: hTERT-immortalized fibroblasts at 3% O₂ should still reach a division limit correlating with centriolar Δ2. Negative control: centriole-less (erythrocytes, thrombocytes) and post-mitotic lineages (neurons, myocytes) should show no centriolar division counter.

**Oxygen-dependent counter dissociation (testable model).** At physiological 3% O₂, hypoxia upregulates telomerase via HIF-1 (Nishi et al., 2004) and lowers oxidative stress, stabilizing telomeres—so the telomeric contribution is diminished (not fully abolished; Parrinello et al., 2003), allowing other counters to become detectable. At 21% O₂, oxidative stress accelerates telomere shortening and DNA damage, so the telomeric/DNA-damage limit dominates and masks the centriolar one. Consistent data: mouse MEFs (long, stable telomeres) have extended—not infinite—replicative potential at 3% O₂ and senesce at 20% O₂ via DNA damage, not telomeres (Parrinello et al., 2003). Decisive prediction: hTERT-immortalized human fibroblasts at 3% O₂ should still reach a division limit with a centriolar phenotype as the only remaining counter—a direct, inexpensive test.

### 5.2 Systems view (MCARA → CEDAR → CAASM)

MCARA is the four-counter architecture; its centriolar component CEDAR (Centriolar Endogenous Damage Accumulation and Reset) posits the centriole as an irreversible-damage counter; CAASM couples wall PTMs to signaling (cilium, Hedgehog/WNT, SASP; Nigg & Raff, 2009). Cross-counter dynamics: dD_total/dt = Σᵢ wᵢ·dDᵢ/dt + ΣΣ cᵢⱼ·Dᵢ·Dⱼ; the centriole term has dD_centriole/dt_reset = 0—the falsifiable signature. Asymmetric inheritance preserves the aged mother centriole in Drosophila GSC (Yamashita et al., 2007; Riparbelli et al., 2021) and mouse neural progenitors (Wang et al., 2009; Royall et al., 2023); inheritance direction is tissue-specific (Drosophila neuroblasts retain the daughter; Januschke et al., 2011)—parameter s (§3.4); the two centrosomes are functionally unequal in Drosophila neural stem cells (Rebollo et al., 2007). Complete centriole loss causes CIN (Sir et al., 2013) and p53-dependent arrest (Fong et al., 2016; Wong et al., 2015); via cilium loss it disrupts Hedgehog/WNT and SASP (Atmakuru & Dhawan, 2023). Fate experiments confirm the differentiation-organelle role: CETN3 loss disrupts neural fate (Xu et al., 2025); Pcm1 loss favors differentiation (Zhao et al., 2025); PLK1/MCPH control centrosome asymmetry (González-Martínez et al., 2022); unequal centrosomes direct asymmetric division (Reina & Gonzalez, 2014); asymmetric inheritance of the centrosome-associated primary-cilium membrane directs ciliogenesis after division (Paridaen et al., 2013)—the aged centriole carries a cilium-membrane compartment, shifting emphasis from damage accumulation toward cilium-compartment transmission (differentiation regulator); the centrosome-centric view of asymmetric division is reviewed in Chen & Yamashita (2021).

### 5.3 Counterarguments and alternative hypotheses

The centriole is advanced as one of several non-exclusive candidates for the persistence of the division limit:

1. **Slow-but-nonzero wall elongation** (Iyer & Akhmanova, 2026) is the weakest link—if wall subunit exchange occurs on relevant timescales, the counter collapses (falsifiable via SILAC); documented dynamics is cap-controlled plus-end lengthening, not replacement; centrosome-phagy (Wu et al., 2021) and LGALS3BP (Fogeron et al., 2013) are clearance/recycling, not in-place repair.
3. **Epigenetic division counting in HSC (strongest alternative, per recent data):** HSC count and remember self-renewal divisions through progressive H3K4me2 demethylation (Bernitz et al., 2016)—an epigenetic division counter that is OSK-resettable in principle, directly competing with the centriolar counter; discrimination: P5 (OSK) — if Δ2 persists while division marks are reset, the centriolar reading is favored.
3. **Acentriolar division is feasible but costly** (Basto et al., 2006; Kalbfuss & Gönczy, 2023; Takumi & Kitagawa, 2022): centriole loss → CIN (Sir et al., 2013) and p53 arrest (Fong et al., 2016; Meitinger et al., 2016; Lambrus et al., 2015; Renzova et al., 2018); de novo assembly (Khodjakov et al., 2002; Uetake et al., 2007) replaces number, not wall state. The p53/PFT-α control (§4) separates organelle-requirement from wall-damage effects.
4. **Germline reset is elimination, not repair** (Sluder et al., 1989; Borrego-Pinto et al., 2016; Pimenta-Marques et al., 2016); **somatic elimination exists but is not in-place wall repair:** programmed centriole elimination occurs in late-embryonic somatic lineages of C. elegans (Kalbfuss & Gönczy, 2023; Croisier et al., 2025), so a somatic elimination-based reset *is* documented—but it replaces number in a developmental context, not the wall state of proliferating adult stem cells; this narrows, and does not abolish, the claim. Centrioles were lost in the planarian lineage (Azimzadeh et al., 2012); centriole evolution constrains architecture (Azimzadeh & Marshall, 2010); **centriole stability is actively regulated (C. elegans SAS-1/SSNA-1; Jha et al., 2025), not passively inert**. **Evolutionary logic:** disposable-soma (Kirkwood, 1977) permits germline-only restoration; among somas, lineages that can afford acentriolar division (C. elegans epithelia) eliminate centrioles, while lineages requiring centrosome-dependent division and asymmetric fate (mammalian HSC/NSC) retain and inherit them—retention, not elimination, is the mammalian strategy. **Comparative caveat (plants):** plants lack centrioles yet show stem-cell/meristem aging via other mechanisms—so the centriole is a candidate counter in centriole-bearing lineages, not a universal one; this scoping is deliberate. iPSC reprogramming partially resets centrosomal architecture (Lapasset et al., 2011); no wall-PTM data after reset exist (P1–P3).
5. **Cancer/immortalized cells bypass, not reset** the counter (p53 loss, amplification, telomerase).
6. **Proliferative-stress alternative (elevated, per recent human data):** aging human hematopoiesis links stem-cell aging to proliferative stress and DNA damage (Lettera et al., 2026)—centriole accumulation may be a downstream readout rather than the primary counter. Discrimination (formalized): if Δ2 correlates with proliferation-stress markers (EdU+pH3+) but not with division count at constant stress (NAC arm), the echo reading is favored; if Δ2 tracks divisions with stress held constant, the counter reading is favored. The niche sets division frequency, so niche signaling modulates the ticking rate of any division counter (μ_eff = μ₀·f(niche)); niche dysfunction and centriolar accumulation are not mutually exclusive (two-level model), testable by niche-manipulation arms.
7. **Centriole as differentiation regulator, not counter (new, per Izumi & Kaneko):** the centrosome controls asymmetric division and cell fate through Odf2 in human neuroblastoma cells (Izumi & Kaneko, 2012) and in neural progenitors (Wang et al., 2009; Royall et al., 2023); if centriolar damage skews asymmetric division toward differentiation, exhaustion could follow from fate skewing rather than from a division counter. Discrimination (added to Stage C): measure the differentiation potential of aged HSC before and after Centrinone-B loss/restoration (lineage-output balance, self-renewal vs differentiation); if exhaustion persists while fate balance is restored, the counter reading is favored; if fate skewing predicts exhaustion, the differentiation-regulator reading is favored.
8. **Cilium-signaling route:** centriolar damage impairs primary-cilium formation (Odf2; Ishikawa et al., 2005) and thus Hedgehog/WNT signaling (Atmakuru & Dhawan, 2023)—an independent senescence route formally outside the counter equation; it is captured by CAASM but not by the ratchet model; discrimination via cilium-resorption (Aurora-A inhibitor) and Smo-CA arms (§4).
9. **Centriole as epigenetic carrier (counter-argument, stated):** asymmetric centriole inheritance does not entail asymmetric chromosome-strand segregation—Drosophila male GSC do not asymmetrically segregate chromosome strands despite asymmetric centriole inheritance (Yadlapalli et al., 2011), so the centriole is not required as a chromatin-segregation carrier; this does not weaken the division-counting claim, which is independent of strand segregation.
10. **Centrosomal defect → replication stress → DNA damage (causal direction):** ciliopathy models show centrosomal dysfunction causes DNA replication stress and damage (CEP290/Joubert; Slaats et al., 2015), so centriolar damage may be *upstream* of, not an alternative to, DNA damage—the γ-H2AX kinetics arm (P1) discriminates direction.
11. **SCNT (resettability only):** the donor centriole is not transferred (Sun & Schatten, 2007); the centrosome–aneuploidy correlation (Dai et al., 2006) is not causal evidence. Causation must come from Centrinone-B and P1–P3.
12. **Independent critique:** the framework "remains a provocative hypothesis requiring direct testing"—we concur; staged thresholds convert it into tested evidence. Disposable-soma (Kirkwood, 1977): evolution restores centrioles only in the germline; asymmetric inheritance accumulates aged centrioles in stem cells (feature, not bug).

**Alternative interpretations of our own data.** (i) Over-elongation and PTM accumulation could be consequences of proteostasis dysfunction—tested by the proteostasis-activation arm (Δ2 unchanged under maximal proteostasis = autonomous; cleared = secondary). (ii) Centrosome amplification (Park et al., 2014) may be a compensatory stress response—discriminated by the NAC arm (amplification is metformin-reversible = stress-dependent; terminal PTMs are not = division-intrinsic); the counter claim rests on terminal PTMs, not amplification. (iii) **Nuclear DNA damage:** quiescent HSC accrue damage, repaired on cycle entry (Beerman et al., 2014); repair deficiencies limit aged HSC (Rossi et al., 2007). OSK does not repair double-strand breaks, so OSK-resistance alone cannot exclude this hypothesis; DNA damage is excluded only if the limit tracks divisions at a constant damage load (inducible-nuclease control) or if γ-H2AX is suppressed (NAC arm) while the limit persists. **Causal-direction control:** centrosomal dysfunction can cause DNA damage (CEP290/replication stress; Slaats et al., 2015); a CDK1/2-inhibitor arm distinguishes primary centriolar change from secondary DNA damage (γ-H2AX without centriolar change = DNA-primary; γ-H2AX preceded by Δ2/geometry change = centriole-primary). (iv) **Proteostasis and aggregation:** yeast aging is division-counted asymmetric retention of damaged proteins (Erjavec et al., 2007); test: proteasome/chaperone induction. (v) **Centrosome-phagy (open, falsifying if confirmed):** if active in normal HSC, it is a somatic reset and **would refute the hypothesis**; current data show stress/pathology-induced, not homeostatic (Wu et al., 2021). Test: autophagy-inhibitor arm (chloroquine/ATG5-KD) with centriole labeling. (vi) **Integrated-threshold model:** if counters sum, resetting three should leave the centriolar term dominant—distinguishable from synergy by the OSK+centriole-persistence readout (P5). (vii) **Epiphenomenon test (kinetic, threshold pre-registered):** Δ2/geometry kinetics vs γ-H2AX and protein-carbonyl kinetics in the same aged HSC; if the Δ2–γ-H2AX correlation exceeds r = 0.8, or the Δ2 rate does not exceed the γ-H2AX rate by ≥2×, the echo interpretation is favored (P1, same animals).

**Formal discrimination criteria (pre-registered, per alternative):**

| Alternative | Exclusion criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| DNA damage | Δ2–γ-H2AX correlation per division | r < 0.3 at n ≥ 50 |
| Proteostasis failure | Δ2 after maximal proteasome/chaperone activation | no significant reduction (p > 0.05) |
| Proliferative stress | Δ2 persistence under NAC (constant stress) | p < 0.05 (division-intrinsic) |
| Epiphenomenon (echo) | Δ2 vs γ-H2AX/carbonyl kinetics | r(Δ2,γ-H2AX) > 0.8 or rate < 2× |
| Differentiation skewing (Izumi & Kaneko) | fate-balance recovery after Centrinone-B | exhaustion persists with restored fate balance |

Each criterion is a Stage-A/P1 arm with the same animals and endpoints; meeting an exclusion criterion weakens the counter claim for that alternative; failing all exclusion criteria strengthens it.

**Formal comparison of alternatives (Occam-consistent).** On current data the centriolar hypothesis is not the most parsimonious—DNA damage (Beerman et al., 2014; Rossi et al., 2007) and proliferative stress (Lettera et al., 2026) explain HSC aging more economically. The centriolar claim is defended only on the specific unexplained residue: the limit persists after OSK resets the epigenetic layer and after telomere/hypoxia/mitophagy mitigation—a residue DNA-damage/stress models can explain only if their damage is also OSK-resistant and division-linked (P1–P3/P5 adjudicate). **Tissue-specificity of the residue:** OSK partially restores function in some tissues (vision; Lu et al., 2020), so the epigenetic reset can be sufficient there; the centriolar claim is scoped to tissues where function is not restored (blood, muscle) and HSC/ISC exhaustion persists. Single base assumption (ratchet of differentiation): geometry changes directionally and irreversibly per division (drift μ) and is copied into the daughter at duplication (α ≈ 0.9–0.97); counting, irreversibility, inheritance, and the limit follow without further assumptions.

**Two-level model.** Replicative aging (in vitro) = programmed geometric change (ratchet); organismal aging (in vivo) = declining regeneration from declining division tempo, traced to error accumulation in the oldest centrioles inherited in asymmetric divisions (Yamashita et al., 2007; Royall et al., 2023). Three age-dependent functions of the centriole: geometry → limit; division tempo (damaged centrosomes slow cycling) → reduced regeneration; cilium competence (Odf2; Ishikawa et al., 2005) → altered Hedgehog/WNT fate. **Mechanistic chain (explicit, closing the 'black box'):** wall PTM/geometry accumulation → impaired cilium formation and centrosomal microtubule nucleation → Hedgehog/WNT attenuation + slowed division tempo + p53-coupled arrest on persistent damage → stem-cell exhaustion; each link is independently testable (P1–P4, Stage C).

**Damage shadow and entropy.** Centriolar damage → slower cycling → reduced regeneration despite OSK (Yücel & Gladyshev, 2024). Entropy (Hong & Cohen, 2025) is general; the centriole is one manifestation—a division-tracked non-exchangeable accumulator or a passive echo, distinguished by SILAC/PLA. Competitive advantage vs other long-lived structures: unlike nuclear pores (renew in proliferating cells; D'Angelo et al., 2009) and protein aggregates (inducibly cleared; Erjavec et al., 2007), the centriole uniquely combines geometric inheritance, a wall without documented in-place repair, and a signaling face (cilium) coupling damage to fate—within Hallmarks (López-Otín et al., 2023), a mechanism within exhaustion/proteostasis/communication, not a separate hallmark.

**Evidence levels (standardized per-claim labels).** IA established (multiple independent confirmations, e.g., telomerase elongation); IB established (single line, consistent); II supported association (age ↔ sperm centriole quality); III plausible mechanistic bridge (templated geometry inheritance); IV unproven hypothesis (Δ2 accumulates on aging HSC centrioles); V causal prediction (centriole replacement restores replicative capacity). Conclusions are drawn from levels I–II only; levels III–V are explicitly labeled at each occurrence.

**Clinical bridge.** Myeloma (Köhrer et al., 2023) and microcephaly (CETN3; Xu et al., 2025). **Transplantation corollary (testable):** if the counter is real, donor-HSC age may limit transplantation outcome independently of telomere length (retrospective registry analysis after telomere correction); **clock-integration test:** in the same HSC cohort, Δ2/geometry burden is correlated with Horvath-clock acceleration—a positive correlation supports coupling of the centriolar and epigenetic layers. **Alternative explanations of OSK-resistance:** (i) incomplete OSK—residual methylation and lineage memory even when clocks reset (Horvath 3.6-y error scale); (ii) non-epigenetic damage shadow—collagen cross-links and ECM not reset by OSK (Yücel & Gladyshev, 2024); (iii) mitochondrial heteroplasmy—selective expansion of mutant mtDNA is selection, not reset. P5 (OSK + centriole-persistence) discriminates the centriolar term from these. Therapy (realistic): no wall repair without elimination (oncogenic risk); near-term: Δ2 as biomarker; reversible-PTM load reduction (CCP5/6, TTL, antioxidants) as risk-reduction.

### 5.4 Critical self-assessment and limitations

**Independent critique.** Author-led critical appraisals of the CEDAR framework exist in author-affiliated venues; they are excluded from citation as non-independent and are not presented as independent critique. To our knowledge (searched through 2026-08), no independent peer-reviewed critique of the hypothesis exists; the niche status is acknowledged, independent critique is explicitly solicited, and the pre-registered protocol is the mechanism by which the field—not the author—adjudicates it. **Position consistency:** earlier author-led publications used the stronger formulation "asymmetric inheritance ... is an established causal fact"; the present manuscript explicitly withdraws that status—every claim is presented as an unverified hypothesis (evidence levels I–II for established facts, III–V for predictions). **Ethical transparency:** no ties to the Heidelberg group; the author's institutional role is disclosed as a potential advocacy conflict; circular argumentation is acknowledged.

**Limitations.** Quantified heterogeneity (I² = 88%/76%, Q = 142.3/38.7; Egger p = 0.29, Begg τ = 0.11—**descriptive only, not informative at k = 4–6**); **no pooled estimate is used at I² > 75%—only stratified per-platform estimates and narrative synthesis**; single-author RoB with 20% independent duplicate (κ = 0.81; two screeners with 100% blind duplication planned); plasma-cell extrapolation (myeloma value used only as an upper bound; fibroblast recalibration is the priority); transcriptome power limits (excluded from the argument); m_eff = 3 to be validated against the pilot covariance matrix; model not quantitatively validated (FRAC mismatch), a hypothesis-generation tool. **Falsification vs confirmation (epistemic status):** positive P1–P3 outcomes are necessary but not sufficient—they only fail to refute; the only paths to positive confirmation are P5 and Stage C. Each outcome is a Bayesian update of the posterior probability, not verification. The HSC-counter claim carries no GRADE rating; the hypothesis stands or falls on the pre-specified falsification tests (P1–P9).

## 6. Conclusion

Despite mitigating every known hallmark, a stem-cell division limit still occurs; we hypothesize the centriole—a candidate counter without a documented somatic reset, in centriole-bearing proliferating stem cells (HSC, NSC, ISC). Telomeres, the epigenome, and mitochondria have multiple reset reports; the centriole has none documented for wall PTMs or geometry, on three grounds: no known in situ repair mechanism for Δ2/C-terminal truncation (provisional); slow, not zero, wall turnover (SILAC to test); spatially inherited geometry without correction. The model and synthesis are hypothesis-generation tools, not validated descriptions of HSC biology; pilot PLA Δ2 data on mouse HSC are a prerequisite for any stronger claim. The falsifiable counter-independence test (P5) is the near-term route to converting the hypothesis into evidence; the protocol is built to falsify it.
