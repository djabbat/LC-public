Dear Editor,

I am writing to ask whether *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* might be interested in an Opinion piece with a somewhat unorthodox thesis.

The core idea is that the centriole — and specifically its acquisition from a phage tubulin ancestor — was the key innovation that made irreversible cell differentiation possible in animals. Bacteria are walled and acentriolar; that much is architectural fact. Some lineages shed the wall in water, giving rise to amoeboid forms. But what turned a colony of modulating cells into a tissue of committed ones? I think the centriole, acting through polyglutamylation and asymmetric inheritance, became a cell-fate tag — and that animals are the lineage that took this tagging system to its logical conclusion.

The argument pulls together several threads that I have not seen connected before: the PhuZ spindle of *Pseudomonas* phages (showing tubulin-based spindles predate eukaryotes), the acentriolar centrosome of *Dictyostelium*, the UNIMOD hypothesis, and the deglutamylase reversibility data. Five alternative models are discussed honestly — I try not to overclaim. Three testable predictions are proposed with concrete protocols.

The manuscript is about 4800 words with 47 references. I believe it fits the Opinion format: it is cross-disciplinary, it is provocative, and it is built to be falsified.

I would be grateful to know whether this falls within the journal's scope.
