Would *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* consider an Opinion article with the following thesis?

**"The Centriole Invasion: How a Phage Tubulin Gave Rise to Irreversible Differentiation"**

The article draws together six observations that, so far as I know, have not been linked before:

1. **Bacteria are walled and acentriolar.** This is an architectural fact, not contingent on where life began.
2. **In permanent water, some lineages shed the wall,** giving rise to wall-less Amoebozoa with acentriolar centrosomes (*Dictyostelium*).
3. **The phage tubulin PhuZ forms a dynamic bipolar spindle,** showing that tubulin-based spindles predate eukaryotes. No horizontal gene transfer was required — regulatory innovation alone suffices.
4. **The centriole became a cell-fate tag:** polyglutamylation and asymmetric inheritance bias which daughter keeps stemness.
5. **True animal tissues** — built of irreversibly committed cells — thus emerged, distinct from the modulated, totipotent cells of bacterial colonies, fungal hyphae, and plant meristems.
6. **Aging is the gradual failure of this tagging system.**

The piece acknowledges the LECA consensus, discusses five alternative models (UNIMOD, autogenous origin, viral eukaryogenesis, spirochete symbiosis, and "centriole as marker, not driver"), reports a negative BLASTp/HHpred search for PhuZ homologs, and proposes three testable predictions with protocols.

~4800 words, 47 references (~30 verified PMIDs).

I think it fits the Opinion format: cross-disciplinary, provocative, and falsifiable.
