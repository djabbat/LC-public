Dear Editor,

Would Biology Direct consider a Hypothesis article with the following thesis?

"The Centriole Invasion: How a Phage Tubulin Gave Rise to Irreversible Differentiation"

The article connects six observations:

1. Bacteria are walled and acentriolar — the ancestral cellular architecture
2. In water, some lineages shed the wall (Amoebozoa with acentriolar centrosomes, e.g., Dictyostelium)
3. Phage tubulin PhuZ forms a dynamic bipolar spindle — tubulin-based spindles predate eukaryotes, requiring only regulatory innovation
4. The centriole became a cell-fate tag: polyglutamylation and asymmetric inheritance bias which daughter keeps stemness
5. True animal tissues emerged from irreversibly committed cells — distinct from the totipotent colonies of bacteria, fungi, and plants
6. Aging is the gradual failure of this tagging system

The argument acknowledges the LECA consensus, evaluates five alternative models (UNIMOD, autogenous origin, viral eukaryogenesis, spirochete symbiosis, "centriole as marker"), reports a negative BLASTp/HHpred search for PhuZ homologs, and proposes three testable predictions with protocols.

~4800 words, 47 references.

I believe it fits the Hypothesis format: cross-disciplinary, provocative, and falsifiable.
