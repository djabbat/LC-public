Since our exchange, I have been digging deeper into the literature on centriole elimination — and I have hit a wall that I suspect only someone in your position can see past.

You mentioned that your lab is working on the mechanism. I am not asking about unpublished results. What I am hoping you might help with is the published landscape. I wonder whether there are studies — old or new, in journals a search engine would not easily surface, perhaps from adjacent fields — that bear on the question of how a somatic cell dismantles its centrioles. The three-stage model you laid out in the Open Biol review is elegant, but the molecular executors remain invisible to someone reading from the outside.

Three specific questions, if you have a moment:

1. Is there work on centriole elimination in the C. elegans soma that is hard to find through PubMed — older literature, developmental studies that mention elimination in passing without using the word, or work from other nematodes?

2. If the mechanism is genuinely unknown to everyone, is there anyone else — another lab, a postdoc who moved on, a group working on centrosome disassembly in a different context — who is actively chasing this?

3. And if the field is truly wide open: where would you tell a student to start? What phenomenon, what precedent, what corner of the literature would you point to as the best foothold?

I ask because the experiment I am designing — centriole elimination in mammalian fibroblasts followed by reprogramming — would benefit enormously from knowing how nature does it, even if only in outline. At present the best model I have is the oocyte: Polo kinase, PCM disassembly, then centriole loss. But the soma in C. elegans does not use the same machinery, or at least not obviously. Any thread you could offer — a paper, a phenomenon, a name — would help.
