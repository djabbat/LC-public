Dear Lukáš,

A short note about an experiment that, as far as I can tell, has never been published. Pierre Gönczy confirmed as much in an email last week.

**The question:** Take a somatic fibroblast. Eliminate its centrioles with Plk4 siRNA or centrinone. Then reprogram it with OSKM. Does reprogramming efficiency change?

I published a theory in 2023 — CEDAR (PMID 36583780). The argument is that the centriole is the cell's hardware. It accumulates polyglutamylation with age and stress. It cannot be repaired the way the epigenome can be reset by OSKM. But it does not simply block reprogramming. The centriole provides a directional signal: its age and molecular state tell the cell where it is on the differentiation trajectory. Across generations, this arrow drives the lineage forward — each division ratchets the cell further along its path.

If this is correct, then removing the centriole removes the directional constraint. The cell loses the arrow that keeps it somatic. Reprogramming should become easier.

**Why your lab:** Your 2018 Stem Cell Reports paper (PMID 30197118) showed that PLK4/STIL-mediated centrosome loss drives differentiation in hPSC. That was the forward experiment — loss of centrioles in pluripotent cells pushes them toward differentiation. The reverse — elimination followed by reprogramming — is the natural next step, and no one has done it.

I have attached a concept note with the proposed design. One detail that may matter: Uetake et al. (2007) showed that normal human cells without centrioles arrest through p38, not p53. If you only block p53 during reprogramming — which is standard — the cells may never get the chance to show the effect. pifithrin-α plus SB203580 is the key.

We are theorists — we have the framework and the logic but no bench. If this is something you would consider looking at, even a brief conversation, I would be grateful.
