**3. Drosophila oocyte: retention of centrioles blocks totipotency**  
Pimenta-Marques A, Bento I, Lopes CAM, Duarte P, Jana SC, Bettencourt-Dias M. "A mechanism for the elimination of the female gamete centrosome in Drosophila melanogaster." Science. 2016;353(6294):aaf4866. PMID 27229142.

Polo kinase and PCM downregulation eliminate centrioles from the female germline. Perturbing this program → centrioles are retained → abnormal divisions → female sterility. Forced centriole retention breaks totipotency.

**4. Planarians: the inversion**  
Li Y, Guo F, Jing Q, Zhu X, Yan X. "Characterisation of centriole biogenesis during multiciliation in planarians." Biol Cell. 2020;112(12):398-408. PMID 32776587.

Planarians are centrosome-free. Neoblasts divide without centrioles. Centrioles appear de novo only in terminally differentiated multiciliated cells. The appearance of a centriole marks the end of plasticity.

**5. Human pluripotent stem cells: centriole loss triggers differentiation**  
Renzova T, Bohaciakova D, Esner M, Pospisilova V, Barta T, Hampl A, Cajanek L. "Inactivation of PLK4-STIL Module Prevents Self-Renewal and Triggers p53-Dependent Differentiation in Human Pluripotent Stem Cells." Stem Cell Reports. 2018;11(4):959-972. PMID 30197118.

PLK4 or STIL inactivation → centrosome loss → reduction of pluripotency markers → p53-dependent differentiation. Centrioles are required to maintain the pluripotent state.

### The Pattern

These five data points — from C. elegans, Drosophila, planarians, and human cells — converge on a single principle:

**The centriole stabilises the current cell state. Remove it, and the cell changes state.**

In pluripotent cells, removing centrioles triggers differentiation (Renzova 2018). In oocytes, retaining centrioles that should be eliminated breaks totipotency (Pimenta-Marques 2016). In C. elegans, centriole elimination locks cells into their differentiated identity. In planarians, the absence of centrioles permits neoblasts to remain plastic; the de novo appearance of centrioles coincides with terminal commitment.

This is not a curiosity of one species. It is a conserved principle: the centriole anchors cellular identity.

### My Question to You

Given your lab's central contributions to centriole biology, I wonder whether you have come across — in your own reading or work — any direct experimental test of the reverse experiment:

**If centrioles are prematurely eliminated from differentiated somatic cells, does that increase their capacity to dedifferentiate?**

The forward experiment (centriole loss in pluripotent cells → differentiation) has been done by Renzova and Cajanek. The retention experiment (keep centrioles in oocyte → sterility) by Pimenta-Marques and Bettencourt-Dias. But the third experiment — eliminate centrioles from fibroblasts, then attempt reprogramming — has not been done, to my knowledge. If it exists, I would be grateful if you could point me to it.

I also wonder whether you know of work on the molecular players of somatic centriole elimination in C. elegans. Your 2023 review lists the three hypothetical stages (maintenance → priming → execution) but notes that the mechanisms are unknown. Has there been any progress since?
